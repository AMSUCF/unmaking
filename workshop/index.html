A WORKSHOP IN FOUR ACTS

Critical (Un)Making Workshop

with Emily K. Johnson and Anastasia Salter

Make a hypertext in Twine, a grammar in Tracery, and a sketch in p5.js. Then hand the grammar to an agent and turn it into an object you can hold: a printed cryptex or flower spinner whose rings are the rules you wrote.

HOW THE ACTS CONNECT

Each act makes something you'll use in the next one. The Twine passage that picks at random in Act I is a tiny grammar. In Act II you write a full grammar in Tracery and flatten it into slots. In Act III that grammar runs inside a p5.js sketch you can click and spin. In Act IV you work with an agent to turn the same grammar into geometry, then print it. Each act ends with an unmaking question from the talk, so we stop and ask what the tool did and what we did.

The times are suggestions. Acts I–III each stand on their own, and Act IV works best after Act II.

BRING

  • A laptop with a current web browser. Everything in the reference files runs offline from this folder.
  • For Act I: index cards, string or yarn, tape, and a marker.
  • For Act IV: a free GitHub account, and Claude Code (claude.ai/code in a browser is enough). No subscription? The act includes a way to work with a free chat assistant instead. OpenSCAD (free) if you want to open the print files yourself.
